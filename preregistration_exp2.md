## Title
colormap Legend Interpretation Under Single vs Dual-Task task

## Description
This study will examine the dark-is-more bias reported in prior single-task colormap studies under divided task. Schloss et al. (2018) found a bias favoring darker-as-more mappings in a single-task setting; we extend this by testing a dual-task scenario that better reflects real-world task demands, where people rarely focus on a single task for extended periods. Participants will judge whether a colormap shows greater values on the left or right side, while legend semantics vary across trials. In dual task, participants will simultaneously monitor a phone chat and respond to messages. We expect the dark-is-more bias to be amplified in the dual-task condition, compared to the single-task condition.

Schloss, K. B., Gramazio, C. C., Silverman, A. T., Parker, M. L., & Wang, A. S. (2018). Mapping color to meaning in colormap data visualizations. IEEE transactions on visualization and computer graphics, 25(1), 810-819.

# Overview
## Hypotheses

The following hypotheses are formed based on a power analysis of a pilot study that used a similar design as this experiment.

Missed Trials

Participants will fail to give a response before the colormap disappears when they fail to interpret the colormap in time. 

H1 (Main effect of task on missed trials):
The percentage of missed trials (MT%) will be predicted by task such that MT% will be higher under the dual-task than under the single-task.

H2 (main effect of label on missed trials):
MT% will be predicted by label such that MT% will be higher under the fewer-up condition than under the greater-up condition.

H3 (Interaction between task and lightness mapping on missed trials):
The effect of lightness mapping on MT% will be moderated by task, such that the MT% gap between the dark-more over the light-more mapping will be larger under dual-task than under single-task.
Specifically, under dual-task, there will be a main effect of lightness mapping on MT% such that MT% is lower under dark-more mapping than under light-more mapping.

H4 (Interaction between task, lightness mapping, and label on missed trials):
The effect of lightness mapping on MT% will be moderated by task and label, such that the interaction between lightness mapping and label will be stronger in the dual-task condition than in the single-task condition.
Within the dual-task condition, we hypothesize a two-way interaction between label and lightness mapping, such that the extent to which MT% is higher under the light-more mapping than under the dark-more mapping will be greater with the greater-up label than with the fewer-up label. Specifically, under the greater-up label, MT% will be higher under the light-more mapping than under the dark-more mapping.

Response Time

Participants will make correct responses faster when they interpret the colormap in time.

H5 (Main effect of lightness mapping on response time):
Response times (RTs) will be predicted by lightness mapping such that RTs will be faster under dark-more mapping than under light-more mapping.

H6 (Main effect of label on response time):
RTs will be predicted by label such that RTs will be faster under high-more label than under low-more label.

H7 (Interaction between lightness mapping and label on response time):
The effect of lightness mapping on RTs will be moderated by label, such that the RT advantage of the dark-more over the light-more mapping will be larger under high-more mapping than under low-more mapping.

H8 (Interaction between task, lightness mapping, and label on response time):
The effect of lightness mapping on RTs will be moderated by task and label, such that the interaction between lightness mapping and label will be stronger in the dual-task condition than in the single-task condition.
Within the dual-task condition, we hypothesize a two-way interaction between label and lightness mapping, such that the extent to which RT is faster under the dark-more mapping than under the light-more mapping will be greater with the greater-up label than with the fewer-up label. Specifically, under the greater-up label, RTs will be faster under the dark-more mapping than under the light-more mapping.

Accuracy

H9 (Main effect of lightness mapping on accuracy):
Accuracy will be predicted by lightness mapping such that accuracy will be higher under the dark-more mapping than under light-more mapping.

H10 (Main effect of label on accuracy):
Accuracy will be predicted by label such that accuracy will be higher under the high-more mapping than under low-more mapping.

Prior work suggests there exists a dark-is-more bias (darker colors are naturally interpreted as representing greater values) and a high-is-more bias (higher positions are naturally interpreted as representing greater values).
We hypothesize that these biases become amplified under divided attention.

Because colormaps are presented briefly and responses must be made within a time limit, designs that do not match participants' expectations may delay interpretation and possibly prevent participants from making a response within the aloted time. These outcomes would be reflected in missed-trial rate and response time, respectively.

Missed-trial rate (MR)

H1 (Three-way interaction on missed trials):
MR will show a three-way interaction between task, lightness mapping, and label. Specifically, the combined effect of lightness mapping and label will be stronger under dual-task than under single-task conditions, such that designs that do not match participants' expectations will be more likely to produce missed trials when attention is divided.
Rationale: When attentional resources are limited, participants rely more strongly on their expectations, and therefore require additional processing to interpret designs that go against their expectations.

We will conduct planned analyses to unpack this interaction.
Within the dual-task condition, MR will show a two-way interaction between lightness mapping and label. Specifically, the effect of lightness mapping will be stronger under the high-more label than under the low-more label. Also, the effect of label will be stronger under the dark-more mapping than under the light-more mapping.
Rationale: When the label is aligned with expectation (high-more), whether the lightness mapping also aligns with expectation will matter more. Similarly, when lightness mapping is aligned with expectation (dark-more), whether the label also aligns with expectation will matter more.

We do not hypothesize a significant interaction within the single-task condition as we expect participants to reach interpretation and make responses in time for most of the trials. 

Further unpacking the interaction, within the dual-task condition and under the high-more label, MR will show an effect of lightness mapping. Specifically, MR will be lower for the dark-more mapping than for the light-more mapping. We do not hypothesize an effect of lightness mapping under the low-more label; the low-more label will be already against the participants' expectations, so whether the lightness mapping aligns with expectation will not matter as much.

Similarly, within the dual-task condition and under the dark-more mapping, MR will show an effect of label. Specifically, MR will be lower for the high-more label than for the low-more label. We do not hypothesize an effect of label under the light-more mapping.

H2 (Interaction between task and lightness mapping on missed trials):
MR will show a two-way interaction between task and lightness mapping. Specifically, the effect of lightness mapping will be stronger under the dual-task condition than under the single-task condition.
Rationale: Expectation mismatch is more likely to produce missed trials when attention is divided.

Further unpacking the interaction, within the dual-task condition, MR will show an effect of lightness mapping. Specifically, MR will be lower for the dark-more mapping than for the light-more mapping. We do not hypothesize an effect of lightness mapping under the single-task condition because we expect participants to make responses in time for most of the trials. 

We do not hypothesize an interaction between task and label on MR as pilot data suggests that lightness mapping is more important than label for MR. 

H3 (Main effect of task on missed trials):
MR will be higher in the dual-task condition than in the single-task condition.
Rationale: When attentional resources are limited, participants will be more likely to fail to interpret the colormap within the time limit.

# Research Design

## Study design
This study will use a mixed factorial design.

Between-subjects factor (random assignment)
1. Task (single or dual).

Within-subject factors:
1. Lightness mapping (dark-more or light-more).
2. Label (greater-up or fewer-up)

------------------------------------

Experimental Procedure:

1. Instructions: Participants will read instructions for the task and will be shown example stimuli. 
2. Practice trials: Participants will complete 20 practice trials designed to introduce the task.
3. Experiment trials: Participants will complete the 80 experiment trials. They are given a short break every 20 trials.
4. Color vision test: Participants will complete a color vision test.

In each trial, participants in the single task condition will perform a colormap task.
Participants in the dual task condition will perform both colormap task and phone task simultaneously.

Colormap task:
A colormap and its corresponding legend will be displayed. We will use a subset of the stimuli used in the experiment of Schloss et al., 2018. We will only use colormaps with a blue-hue color scale and with a white background.  
The legend will be a vertical color gradient and will be positioned next to the colormap. The legend will be different based on the lightness mapping and label conditions. 
In the dark-more lightness mapping condition, the darker colors will map to greater values. In the light-more lightness mapping condition, the lighter colors will map to greater values.
In the greater-up label condition, the label "greater" is at the top of the legend. In the fewer-up label condition, the label "fewer" is at the top of the legend.
Participants will be asked to look at the colormap and its legend to indicate which side (left or right) of the colormap represents greater values. They will respond in left or right arrow keys.
On incorrect response, a red "Incorrect" message is shown for 1000 ms before proceeding to the next trial.

Phone task (dual task condition only):
A phone display will run concurrently to the left of the colormap display. A stream of messages will appear on the phone screen. The messages are meant to simulate those that appear on a chat room of a group of friends. A message will be either a message that is related or unrelated to a pet (dog, cat, parrot, or goldfish). A message will be displayed for 3 seconds and disappear, with the next message showing up arbitrarily between 0.5 and 2 seconds.
Participants will be asked to look at the phone and "like" pet-related messages by pressing spacebar. Each phone response will be scored into hit, miss, false alarm, or correct rejection.
If participant "likes" a pet-related message, the background of the message will turn green.
If participant "likes" a pet-unrelated message, the background of the message will turn red.
If participant does not "like" a pet-related message in time, an angry emoji will be displayed briefly.

Color vision test:
Participants will perform an Ishihara color-vision test (11 items) and will answer a question about their color vision.

Reference:
Schloss, K. B., Gramazio, C. C., Silverman, A. T., Parker, M. L., & Wang, A. S. (2018). Mapping color to meaning in colormap data visualizations. IEEE transactions on visualization and computer graphics, 25(1), 810-819.

## Randomization (Optional)
We will use 20 distinct colormaps. 10 will be darker on the left side and the other 10 will be darker on the right side.
Each of the 20 colormap will be used to create 4 stimuli by altering the legend, based on lightness mapping and label conditions.
This will result in a stimuli pool of 80 stimuli. The order of 80 stimuli are shuffled per participant.
Practice trials will use colormaps not in the stimuli pool.

200 pet-unrelated phone messages and 80 pet-related messages will be generated before the experiment.
The pet-unrelated phone messages will be divided into conversations. Each conversation will contain 10 messages.
The messages within a conversation will share a common theme, so that the dialogue can flow naturally.
For each participant, the order of the conversations will be randomized, while the order of messages within each conversation will remain unchanged. Messages will then be displayed sequentially. In 40% of trials, a message will instead be randomly sampled from the pool of 80 pet-related messages and presented to the participant. If all 200 pet-unrelated messages are displayed, the sequence will restart from the beginning.
The number of messages viewed by each participant will vary depending on how quickly they complete the colormap task.

## Data collection procedures

## Sample size
Our target sample size is 80 participants.

## Sample size rationale (Optional)
We chose the sample size of 80 participants based on a power analysis using data from a pilot study, which used the same design as we will use here. We used the R mixedpower library to conduct a power analysis. The power analysis suggested that 40 participants per task condition are needed to obtain .80 power to detect the interaction between lightness mapping and task.

## Starting and stopping rules
We will initially post the study on Prolific with 80 spots (40 spots per task condition) . After excluding data based on the exclusion criteria, if there are task conditions that have less than 40 participants, we will keep posting 3 additional spots for those conditions until all the task conditions have at least 40 participants.

# Variables

## Manipulated variables
1. Task (single and dual)
   - Single: Participant only performs the colormap task
   - Dual: Participant performs both colormap task and phone task simultaneously
2. Lightness mapping (dark-more and light-more)
   - Dark-more: Darker colors map to greater values
   - Light-more: Lighter colors map to greater values
3. Label: (high-more and low-more)
   - High-more: “Greater” appears at the top of the legend
   - Low-more: “Fewer” appears at the top of the legend

## Measured variables
Colormap task:
1. Accuracy: For each trial, the correctness of the participant's response will be measured.
2. Response Time: For each trial, the time taken between the display of the stimulus and the participant's response will be measured. 
3. Block Number: For each participant, trials will be divided into four sequential blocks of 20 based on their order of presentation. Each trial will be labeled as belonging to Block 1, 2, 3, or 4.

Phone task (only in dual task condition):
1. Hit: For each participant, the number of pet-related messages they "liked" during the whole experiment will be measured.
2. Miss: For each participant, the number of pet-related messages they did not "like" during the whole experiment will be measured.
3. False Alarm: For each participant, the number of pet-unrelated messages they "liked" during the whole experiment will be measured.
4. Correct Rejection: For each participant, the number of pet-unrelated messages they did not "like" during the whole experiment will be measured.

## Indices
- colormap accuracy: mean of `correct`.
- colormap RT: mean `duration` (or `response_time` fallback), primarily on correct trials.
- Phone sensitivity: d' with 95% CI using Gourevitch & Galanter-style variance approximation.

# Analysis Plan

## Statistical models
To test the hypotheses regarding missed-trial rate (MR), we will fit a Generalized Linear Mixed Effects Regression (GLMER) model. We will predict MR of a trial from fixed effects of task (between-subject) * lightness mapping (within-subject) * label (within-subject). We will include random by-subject slopes and intercepts for each within-subject fixed effect.

To test the hypotheses regarding response times (RT), we will fit a Linear Mixed Effects Regression (LMER) model. We will predict RT of a correct trial from fixed effects of task (between-subject) * lightness mapping (within-subject) * label (within-subject). We will include random by-subject slopes and intercepts for each within-subject fixed effect.

For both models, if we find a three-way interaction, we will conduct the same model within dual-task(excluding task from the model) to examine two-way interactions between lightness mapping and label. 

If we find a two-way interaction, we will conduct pairwise comparisons by conducting the same model within dark-more mapping (excluding lightness mapping from the model) and within high-more label (excluding label from the model):
i. Within the dark-more mapping, we will predict MR and RT from a fixed effect of label. We will include random by-subject slopes and intercepts for label.
ii. Within the high-more label, we will predict MR and RT from a fixed effect of lightness mapping. We will include random by-subject slopes and intercepts for lightness mapping.

To test the hypotheses regarding accuracy, we will fit a Generalized Linear Mixed Effects Regression (GLMER) model. We will predict accuracy of a trial from fixed effects of task (between-subject) * lightness mapping (within-subject) * label (within-subject). We will include random by-subject slopes and intercepts for each within-subject fixed effect.

If we find a two-way interaction, we will examine the simple effects of lightness mapping under label and label under lightness mapping, by fitting the same model separately within each lightness mapping condition (excluding lightness mapping from the model) and within each label condition (excluding label from the model).

## Transformations
Categorical variables (task, lightness mapping, and label) will be contrast-coded (−0.5, 0.5). 
For each trial where the participant provided a response within the time limit, accuracy will be recorded as 1 (correct) or 0 (incorrect). An accurate response is when the participant correctly indicates the side (left/right) of the colormap that represents greater values.
For each trial, missed trial will be recorded as 1 (missed) or 0 (answered). A missed trial is when the participant did not respond to the colormap task before the colormap disappears.

## Inference criteria
We will use a  p&lt;.05 criterion for determining whether effects from the GLMER models are significant.

## Data inclusion and exclusion
1. Exclude participants with low colormap task accuracy
Participants with fewer than 50 correct responses out of 80 trials will be excluded, following a binomial test threshold with α = 0.05. 

2. Exclude participants with low phone task sensitivity (dual task condition only)
Participants whose approximated 95% confidence interval of sensitivity (d') includes 0 will be excluded.

## Missing data
Participants will be excluded from analysis if they do not complete the entire experiment. 
Models will be conducted on all available data and otherwise ignore whether data is missing.

## Other planned analysis (Optional)
We will also analyze the fixed effects that are part of the planned LMER and GLMER model above but that are not part of the hypotheses. If there are two-way interactions between task and lightness mapping or between task and label, we will unpack those interactions within each task by excluding task from the model. If there are two-way interactions between lightness mapping and label, we will unpack those interaction within each lightness mapping and within each label. 

We will also extend the both LMER and GLMER model used in the main and exploratory analyses to include block number to examine order effects. Block number will be added as a fixed effect, along with random by-subject slopes and intercepts. Block number will be coded using centered linear contrasts (−1.5, −0.5, 0.5, 1.5). We expect that the accuracy will increase and the response time and missed-trial rate will decrease over each block.
